## Stories / Scenarios

Stories/Scenarios:

- Create one scenario around a potential project with your partner(s).
  - Seek feedback on the idea, but first add a bit more detail regarding actors and context before reaching out.
  - If you do not have your own idea, use AI to generate a scenario. It is still for practice and does not need to become your final project.
- Present the story/scenario to a neighboring team (2×5 minutes).
- Discuss potential problems/challenges (5 min) and take notes.
- Discuss implementation possibilities (10 min): how likely can you build something like this with the kit and other hardware available in class, what would need to be mocked up, and what kind of integration would be needed?

Remember:

- It needs to be playable and pitchable.
- AI can be incredibly helpful in this task, but the result will most likely have to be refined and adjusted by you.

## Final Project

Ideas for final projects can be found here:
[https://docs.google.com/document/d/1teqr0nhfCHbBuo15FlxK9HuAG6_qJZO3SNs4zY_-MCs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1teqr0nhfCHbBuo15FlxK9HuAG6_qJZO3SNs4zY_-MCs)

The **syllabus is the single source of truth** for final-project assessment and minimum requirements. The following checklist summarizes those requirements:

- The challenge should be meaningful for at least one team member, ideally more.
- Form a team of 4–6 people and document individual roles and contributions in each personal portfolio.
  - Use Scrum if it helps your team and you already know the method; otherwise it might be overkill.
- Cross at least two domains (for example, environmental monitoring, security, remote access, or home automation).
- Create a story, including at least one pitchable story.
- Use IoTempower where appropriate; discuss alternatives with the instructor.
- Build a multi-node system in which components interact through an integrator:
  - at least 6 nodes or mobile/wireless devices
  - at least two different sensor types and one actuator across 3 nodes
  - at least one gateway with MQTT
  - an integration layer, for example Node-RED
  - a dashboard
  - documented architecture
  - optional simulated components and cloud connection

## Final Project Presentation

Present your final project through a live demonstration or video presentation, as agreed during the course. Make the agreed assessment evidence available to the instructor through the LMS or another approved channel; public sharing with classmates is welcome but optional.

Presentation expectations include:

- prototypes are preferred (you can use cardboard and/or 3D printing)
- prepare a visual aid: poster or slides
- tell a presentable story
- include portfolio notes and guides about the architecture, technical challenges, and other challenges
- for a video presentation:
  - use an opening slide only briefly to introduce the project
  - continue with a pitch-style playthrough of the running prototype, then add technical explanation; slides may outline or mock parts, but focus on the physical prototype presentation
  - provide a short technical breakdown, supported by slides if useful, showing challenges, successes, and particularly effective solutions
  - end with a positive takeaway and why someone—including you—should continue the project beyond this class
