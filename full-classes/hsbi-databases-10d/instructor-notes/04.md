## What to expect

The most satisfying session for students: they write a rule once and watch the database *refuse* bad data. The reframe that makes it land: **an error message here is the feature**, not a failure. Say it early.

The trap that catches almost everyone: **`PRAGMA foreign_keys` is per connection and OFF by default.** A student will create the foreign key, insert `sensor_id = 99`, and see it succeed. Do not fix it for them — ask what they think happened, then show the pragma. It is the best five minutes of the session.

Two other surprises worth predicting: `CHECK` constraints **pass `NULL`** (a `CHECK (value > 0)` does not reject a null value), and `STRICT` rejects `'hot'` in a `REAL` column but happily accepts `'42'` as text that looks numeric — depending on the column type.

## Checkpoint 1 — a feedback round

You are not grading today. Nothing is recorded as a result; the graded assessment is at the end. Say that in the first minute, or the pods will perform instead of showing you what is actually there.

**The arithmetic.** 2 × 90 = 180 minutes. The peer practice takes 35, leaving 145 for 12 pods — 12 minutes each, with one to spare.

| Minutes | What |
|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Late-start buffer (Deutsche Bahn) |
| 0:05–0:17 | Peer practice: Pod A presents to Pod B — 12 min |
| 0:17–0:20 | Switch and write feedback notes — 3 min |
| 0:20–0:32 | Peer practice: Pod B presents to Pod A — 12 min |
| 0:32–0:35 | Reflections and settling — 3 min |
| 0:35–2:59 | 12 pods × 12 min with you; everyone else keeps working |
| 2:59–3:00 | Close: what happens next, when points are recorded, what "not yet" means |

**The 12-minute slot:** 2 min — "show me the one artefact you are proudest of, and name the goal it proves"; 2 min — "now the other module: show it, or say 'not yet'"; 3 min — one question, one strength, one next step; 2 min — write the line; 3 min — transition. An unprepared pod gets **4 minutes**: "not yet", one next step, move on.

**The peer round does the portfolio gate.** Every member opens *their own* portfolio on *their own* device; the receiving pod checks that it is their own copy, that they can edit, commit, and link — and then each member shows **one artefact** and the pod shows **one reflection** it is proud of. Each direction gets **12 minutes**, with a 3-minute switch for writing feedback notes. Questions to feed the receiving pod are in the manual.

Full run sheet, the question bank, and the fill-in template: **[feedback-round manual](./feedback-rounds.md)**. A podmate who is ill can join by video call, get the recording, send a short message, or take a 15-minute office-hour slot — see [If someone is missing](./feedback-rounds.md#if-someone-is-missing).

## Opening (5 minutes)

Story first: a disconnected sensor reports 9,000 °C, and there is no DBA at the edge to notice. Then: *"Today the database refuses to store it, and you will watch it refuse."* That promise is what the acceptance test delivers.

## Running the studio

- **Task A (acceptance test, 15 min).** The point is contrast: the unconstrained table takes all three junk rows; the constrained one rejects them one layer at a time. Ask pods to name **which layer** rejected each row, not just the error.
- **Task 1 (the four rejections).** Run them **one at a time**; the shell stops at the first error in batch mode. Record the exact messages — they are portfolio evidence.
- **Task 2 (UPSERT).** The test is "run it twice, count the rows". Make them prove the count, not assert it.
- **Task 3 (triggers).** The vibration trigger is the crowd-pleaser: insert 2.7 mm/s, see an alarm row appear. Then ask the cost question — a trigger on a hot ingest path is a design decision.
- **Task 4 (DCL).** If time is short, demo `PRAGMA query_only` and a read-only connection in two minutes; skip the chmod part.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Foreign keys "not working"** — the pragma. See above.
- **`STRICT` on an existing table.** You cannot add it with `ALTER`; students must recreate the table. Worth showing once.
- **Trigger fires twice.** `AFTER UPDATE` on a multi-row `UPDATE` fires per row; if they expect one audit row, they will be surprised. That is a good question to leave open.
- **Students adding constraints to the legacy schema from Module 2** and finding orphan rows block the migration. Excellent teaching moment: clean the data first, then add the constraint.

## Read up on

- [`STRICT` tables](https://www.sqlite.org/stricttables.html), [generated columns](https://www.sqlite.org/gencol.html), [triggers](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_createtrigger.html), [`UPSERT`](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_upsert.html).
- [Foreign keys in SQLite](https://sqlite.org/foreignkeys.html) — especially the "foreign key enforcement is off by default" section.
- SQL three-valued logic, if `CHECK` and `NULL` surprise you too.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations.** Nudge them toward: `PRAGMA foreign_keys` sitting inside the setup script, `CHECK (value > 0)` accepting `NULL`, and the trigger that fires more often than they expected. Swap at the break and at the end.
