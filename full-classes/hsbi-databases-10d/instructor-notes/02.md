## What to expect

The first session with **no tooling**, and it feels completely different: the room gets quiet, then argumentative. That is good. Students want to draw boxes immediately; the pod study exists so they draw *better* boxes ten minutes later.

The "aha" you are listening for is the many-to-many hidden inside `parts_used = 'P-1001,P-1002'`. When a pod says it out loud, stop the room and ask them to repeat it.

Expect two failure directions in the ER diagrams: **over-specification** (a cardinality of `1:4` where the domain only supports `1:n`) and **under-specification** (one box labelled "database"). Both are worth a minute at the board.

## Opening (5 minutes)

Use the story, not a summary: Anke Reuter asks a question that should take a minute and takes an afternoon. Then one sentence: *"Today you are the person who fixes that."* Then straight into the pod study — do not preview the concepts, that is what the study is for.

## Running the studio

- **Task A (30 min study).** Circulate. The most useful intervention is "where did you read that?" — the study log requires a source for every claim.
- **Task B (four sketches).** Do this *inside* the study block; it is where the learning happens. Ask each pod to defend one sketch against a neighbouring pod before drawing more.
- **Task C (15 min share).** Enforce the output: 3–5 agreed points and **one image** posted to Discord. The single shared image matters — it is the artefact the room remembers.
- **Task 1 (ER reconstruction).** Expect the `Technician` question. Honest answer: it is an entity if it has its own attributes and lifecycle, an attribute if it is just a name; the schema stores a name, which is exactly the defect.
- **Task 2 (five defects).** Insist on **proof by query**, not a list. This is the professional habit: "I can show you" beats "I think".
- **Task 3 (contract).** Non-programmers slow down here. Pair them; it is about the *shape* of the contract, not Pydantic fluency.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Students repair the schema instead of auditing it.** The deliverable is an audit of what exists plus a contract for what it should be. Say it twice.
- **Diagrams as decoration.** Require the cardinality on every edge; an unlabelled line is not a model.
- **The 1:4 cardinality.** A specific number on an edge is usually a modelling error — the domain says "one machine has sensors", not "four".
- **Pods skip the LLM-verification column.** Mark it: an LLM answer without a check is not evidence.

## Read up on

- ER and EER models, and Crow's-foot notation — the [resource bank](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#visualizing-an-er-model-mermaid-diagramsnet) has the rendering tools.
- First normal form, and why a comma-separated list breaks it.
- Pydantic and SQLAlchemy at the "define a model" level — enough to recognise a good contract.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations.** Nudge them toward: the comma-separated `parts_used` column, the duplicate `P-1003` row, `unit_price` stored as TEXT, and whichever ER diagram from their search looked strangest. Swap at the break and at the end.
