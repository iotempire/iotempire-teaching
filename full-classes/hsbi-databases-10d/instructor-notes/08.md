## What to expect

The room is nervous, the network is unreliable, and every team believes it needs a stage slot. Your
job is timekeeping and fairness, not teaching. The fair format replaces the 15-minute stage talk:
each team stands at a table with a live demo, a poster or a few slides, and a 2-minute recorded
fallback. Teams present while the other lane visits; then the lanes swap.

Three predictable events:

1. **A live demo crashes.** The module requires a fallback recording. A crash handled calmly and
   explained is fine; a crash nobody can explain costs the team its demonstration points.
2. **One member cannot answer.** “That was my teammate’s part” is explicitly not an answer. Record
   it as an individual note — it does not sink the team, but it matters.
3. **Demos built on slides.** Stop them politely and ask for the query. The rubric says “slides may
   support, never replace, the live run”.

## Before the session

- [ ] Post the **table/lane map** and the order in which visitors rotate. Tell each team where its
      table is and which lane it presents in first.
- [ ] Clone every repository onto the demo machine beforehand; do not debug Git during a
      discussion.
- [ ] Print or open the rubric with a column per team; write individual notes as you go.
- [ ] Check the projector with a **terminal** — the demo is text, and small fonts are the usual
      failure.
- [ ] Brief the TA on the lane, the questions, the notes, and the rule: **feedback, not grades**.
- [ ] Ask teams to bring power strips and a 2-minute fallback video; the room is a fair, not a
      single demo machine.

## The fair run sheet (180 minutes)

| Time | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–15 | Setup: tables, laptops, posters, videos, projector/network check |
| 15–75 | **Block 1:** lane A presents; lane B visits. Instructor at lane A; TA at lane B |
| 75–90 | Break |
| 90–150 | **Block 2:** lane B presents; lane A visits. Instructor at lane B; TA at lane A |
| 150–165 | Whole-room wrap: one surprising finding per lane; note follow-ups |
| 165–180 | Retrospective / buffer |

Each lane has five teams. The instructor spends about 12 minutes per table in its presenting block:
a short live demo, the plan/timing proof, the integrity demo, and one question per member. The TA
visits the other lane and writes feedback; the instructor reviews the notes and owns the grade.
Every team gets instructor time in one block and TA feedback in the other.

## Asking project-discussion questions

One question per member, aimed at the piece they own. Have a short list ready per role:

- **Schema owner:** "Which constraint would you add first if you had one more hour, and what bad row
  does it stop?"
- **Ingestion owner:** "Run the loader twice. What guarantees no duplicates?"
- **Query owner:** "Show the plan. What did the index change, and what did it not change?"
- **Interface owner:** "What happens when a row is missing or malformed?"
- **Docs owner:** "Rebuild it from the README, out loud. Which step is missing?"
- **Anyone:** "What would you change if you started over, and why?"

## Scoring

Use the [rubric](../modules/08-final-project.md) as written — **3.0 code and system documentation,
2.0 demo / discussion**. Do not re-derive the criteria at the fair; consistency across teams matters
more than precision on any one.

Write the individual-discussion note next to the team score. That note, plus the checkpoints and the
portfolio, is the basis for the final conversation. The TA may suggest a demo/discussion outcome;
the instructor records it.

## Retrospective (10 minutes, after the last table)

Three questions, written, one per person, anonymous if they prefer:

1. What did this course make you understand that a lecture would not have?
2. Where did the studio format waste your time?
3. What should the next cohort do differently in week one?

Read them before you plan the next run. The syllabus promises the class moves; this is where you
collect the evidence for how.

## Where it goes wrong

- **The fair becomes chaos.** Without a lane map and a visitor rotation, one table gets a crowd and
  another gets none. Publish the map and keep the rotation moving.
- **One team hogs the instructor.** Keep the 12-minute table visit; the TA handles the queue.
- **Grading the polish.** Posters and production value are not in the rubric; a working query and a
  clear explanation are.
- **Skipping the individual part** because the team demo went well. The individual project
  discussion is where the learning goals are actually verified.
- **The TA assigns points.** The TA gives feedback and suggests; the instructor records.
- **No record.** If you do not write the note during the fair, you will reconstruct it from
  impression three weeks later, which is exactly the sieve this course is trying to avoid.
