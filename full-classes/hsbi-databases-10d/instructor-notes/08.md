## What to expect

The room is nervous, the network is unreliable, and every team believes it needs 25 minutes. Your job is timekeeping and fairness, not teaching.

Three predictable events:

1. **A live demo crashes.** The module already requires a fallback recording. A crash handled calmly and explained is fine; a crash nobody can explain costs the team its demonstration points.
2. **One member cannot answer.** "That was my teammate's part" is explicitly not an answer. Record it as an individual note — it does not sink the team, but it matters.
3. **Demos built on slides.** Stop them politely and ask for the query. The rubric says "slides may support, never replace, the live run".

## Before the session

- [ ] Post the running order, and tell each team the exact minute they start.
- [ ] Clone every repository onto the demo machine beforehand; do not debug Git during a defense.
- [ ] Print or open the rubric with a column per team; write the individual notes as you go, not from memory afterwards.
- [ ] Check the projector with a *terminal* — the demo is text, and small fonts are the usual failure.

## The 15 minutes

| Minutes | Segment |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | The story: the person, the pain, where the data comes from |
| 2–8 | Live end-to-end: raw data → ingest → database → headline query → interface |
| 8–10 | The proof: `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` before/after, the timings, the injection self-test |
| 10–12 | Integrity: trigger the constraint live and show it rejecting bad data |
| 12–15 | Individual defense |

Hold the clock visibly. At minute 8, if they are still on slides, say "show me the query" — you are doing them a favour.

## Asking defense questions

One question per member, aimed at the piece they own. Have a short list ready per role:

- **Schema owner:** "Which constraint would you add first if you had one more hour, and what bad row does it stop?"
- **Ingestion owner:** "Run the loader twice. What guarantees no duplicates?"
- **Query owner:** "Show the plan. What did the index change, and what did it not change?"
- **Interface owner:** "What happens when a row is missing or malformed?"
- **Docs owner:** "Rebuild it from the README, out loud. Which step is missing?"
- **Anyone:** "What would you change if you started over, and why?"

## Scoring

Use the [rubric](../modules/08-final-project.md) as written — 2.0 functionality and measurement, 1.5 architecture and documentation, 1.5 demo and defense. Do not re-derive the criteria in the room; consistency across teams matters more than precision on any one.

Write the individual-defense note next to the team score. That note, plus the checkpoints and the portfolio, is the basis for the final conversation.

## Retrospective (10 minutes, after the last team)

Three questions, written, one per person, anonymous if they prefer:

1. What did this course make you understand that a lecture would not have?
2. Where did the studio format waste your time?
3. What should the next cohort do differently in week one?

Read them before you plan the next run. The syllabus promises the class moves; this is where you collect the evidence for how.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Running long.** Two teams overrunning eats the last team's defense. Keep the clock even if it feels harsh.
- **Grading the polish.** Slides and production value are not in the rubric; a working query and a clear explanation are.
- **Skipping the individual part** because the team demo went well. The individual defense is where the learning goals are actually verified.
- **No record.** If you do not write the note during the session, you will reconstruct it from impression three weeks later, which is exactly the sieve this course is trying to avoid.
