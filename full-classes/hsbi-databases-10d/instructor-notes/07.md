## What to expect

One project, one Task. The module is short; the work is in the room. Session 8 opens with a 5–10 minute three-slide overview (what the modules gave, what the project asks, today's steps), then teams merge, pitch the 6b project tickets, choose a story, map the must-haves, and start the slice. The checkpoint lane runs in the second block while the rest build. Sessions 9–10 are build, performance clinic, peer review, and rehearsal. Session 11 is the fair.

The dominant failure is **breadth-first building**: five half-finished features, no end-to-end path, and a demo that shows slides instead of data. Kill it in Session 8 by insisting on one vertical slice. The second failure is **scope creep dressed as ambition**: a team adding authentication in week 10 will not rehearse.

## Session 8 — kickoff and first slice

### First 90 minutes

| Time | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–5 | Register pods for checkpoint slots (TA) while the instructor starts |
| 5–15 | **Three kickoff slides** ([deck](../slides/07-project-kickoff.md)): what the modules gave, what the project asks, today's steps. Five to ten minutes, then hand off |
| 15–30 | Merge two pods; each pod pitches its 6b project tickets in 60 seconds |
| 30–45 | Choose one story; answer the four questions. **Story test:** no person + first query = topic. Elect the **Product Owner** and the **process keeper**; in teams above six, name the **pod leads** |
| 45–60 | `docs/charter.md`: actors, workflows with states, use cases → queries, the slice, the cut list. Then `docs/backlog.md`: work packages ≤ half a day, each with a delivery, an owner, and an acceptance line |
| 60–90 | Schema sketch, first query, start the vertical slice |

**If a team cannot decide by minute 30**, send them to the [student catalogue](../modules/07-project-catalogue.md); do not let them browse. The project ticket is the input, not a new brainstorm.

**Screen every pitch** with the self-check in the [student catalogue](../modules/07-project-catalogue.md#4-does-your-project-pass); the verdict rules are in the [idea bank §2](./project-ideas.md#2-verdicts): green → L2; amber → build the L1 slice first and turn the missing qualities into work packages for Session 9; red → send them to a family. One line per team on the kickoff sheet: team, person, decision, query, rows, second actor, state, verdict. The TA can run the screen for a team that arrives late or undecided while the instructor keeps the room moving.

### The team process (Sessions 8–11)

A light version, not full Scrum: two roles, one board, and a short ceremony per session — 3 minutes
per pod, then a 2-minute leads sync, and a 3-minute review per pod. Nothing about the process is
graded; the plan point is earned by the board and the review evidence.

- **Product Owner:** owns `docs/backlog.md`, priorities, one acceptance line per work package, and
  the cut list. Prioritises; does not assign.
- **Process keeper:** runs the stand-up, keeps the board honest, watches the commit log, escalates
  blockers. Reports blockers, not people.
- **Pods as units:** the Task Pod stays the working unit. Each pod owns one or two deliveries —
  the slice, the proof, or the surface — and names a pod lead. The board allows at most two items in
  *doing* per pod and one per person.
- **The 48-hour rule:** a work package in *doing* with no commit for 48 hours goes to the TA the
  same day. Check it in Session 9, not at the fair.
- **Review:** the last 10 minutes of Sessions 9 and 10 — each pod demos its delivery in 3 minutes;
  the Product Owner accepts it or sends it back.

If a team arrives in Session 8 without roles, give them five minutes to elect the two, then move on.
Do not let the election become a discussion.

### Second 90 minutes

| Time | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–50 | **Checkpoint 2, first half:** two lanes (instructor + TA), five pods each × 10 min, while teams build |
| 50–90 | Build the vertical slice: schema + FK/CHECK → idempotent `ingest.py` → headline query → interface |

The checkpoint is feedback, not a grade. Pods register at the start of the day; the TA writes the notes and may suggest points/readiness and mark a recheck; the instructor records.

## Session 9 — build and performance clinic

- **First block:** Checkpoint 2, second half — the other ten pods, two lanes, 10 minutes each, while teams build. Registration at the start.
- **Second block:** performance clinic, 30 minutes. Three teams bring their slowest query; profile on the projector with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`, the index that fixes it (or does not), and the before/after timing. Then build.
- The TA floats; the instructor walks and asks the three coaching questions.

## Session 10 — harden, review, rehearse

- **Stand-up:** the one must-have each team finishes today.
- **Harden:** index proof, injection sweep, trigger/constraint demonstration.
- **Peer review:** trade repositories; rebuild from the README in three commands; find one missing constraint and one unparameterized query. The reviewers commit the fixes.
- **Rehearse the fair table:** the relay (about a minute per member) plus the end-to-end path, then 5 minutes of questions; one question per member; record the 2-minute fallback. Cut slides, not demo time.

## Session 11 — the fair

**Before class:** the first block's tables are set up with laptops, posters/slides, power, and the 2-minute fallback. **During the break:** the remaining teams set up.

| Block | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Block 1** (90 min) | Up to five tables × 15 min; with seven teams, four tables = 60 min and 30 min buffer. Instructor + TA circulate. Non-presenting students visit and write their final reflection. |
| **Break** (15 min) | The remaining teams set up |
| **Block 2** (90 min) | Same format for the remaining teams |

Each table gets **10 minutes live demo + 5 minutes of questions**, hard clock. In a team of 7–9 the demo is a relay: each member takes the part they own for about a minute, then the end-to-end path runs. Keep each answer in the question round to about 30 seconds; take the depth from the relay. The instructor and TA move together in both blocks — these are the TA's two training rounds. At every table: one thing that impressed you, then the question that matters. Use the syllabus rubric as written; do not re-derive it at the fair. The TA may suggest points and mark a recheck; the instructor records.

**Contribution:** each member names the part they built, tested, reviewed, debugged, or presented;
a teammate confirms it; the TA writes it down. Small parts count fully — acting in the demo, the
fallback video, the README, an AI suggestion rejected with evidence. “I was in the meetings” does
not. The individual discussion points depend on this (see the syllabus).

**Questions by role:** schema — “Which constraint would you add first, and what bad row does it stop?”; ingestion — “Run the loader twice. What guarantees no duplicates?”; query — “Show the plan. What did the index change?”; interface — “What happens when a row is missing or malformed?”; docs — “Rebuild it from the README, out loud.”; anyone — “Why does this matter to you?”; anyone — “What would you change if you started over?”; anyone — “Show me the worst thing an AI gave you in this repo, and what you changed.”

**Visitors:** visit at least three tables per block; write one thing that impressed you, one question you asked, and one concrete suggestion. Use the notes in the final reflection. The visit is part of the learning, not a break.

**After the last table:** a 10-minute retrospective — what did this course make you understand that a lecture would not? Where did the project format waste your time? What should the next cohort do differently in week one?

## TA role

- **Session 8:** register pods, help undecided teams, run a checkpoint lane.
- **Sessions 9–10:** second checkpoint lane, then float for setup and debugging.
- **Session 11:** join the instructor for both fair blocks; ask one question per member; write feedback; suggest points/readiness and mark rechecks. Never record the grade.
- A pod stuck for more than ten minutes, a student who cannot explain their part, or a broken demo is an escalation, not a rescue.

## Where it goes wrong

- **No story.** A team with no named person builds generic CRUD. Send them back to the project ticket or the catalogue.
- **Breadth-first building.** Five half-features, no end-to-end path. Force one slice in Session 8.
- **Data committed to Git.** `.db`, `.parquet`, large CSVs, and credentials stay out; the script is the deliverable.
- **One person doing everything.** Visible in the commit log within ten minutes. Intervene in Session 8, not at the fair.
- **The ghost member.** No board item, no commit, no part at the table. Check `docs/team.md` and the commit log in Session 9; the individual discussion is where it costs points.
- **The silent nine.** In a team of 7–9, three people can disappear. The pod lead names every member's item at the Session 9 sync; check the contribution log then, not at the fair.
- **The committee.** Everyone decides, nobody owns. Force one Product Owner and one name per work package.
- **The AI dump.** One commit of generated code with no `docs/ai-log.md`. Ask for the rejected suggestion in Session 9, not at the fair.
- **No interface until Session 10.** Force `datasette` in Session 8; it takes one command and removes a whole class of failure.
- **The demo runs long on slides.** Rehearse the live path; cut slides, not demo time.
- **The checkpoint eats the kickoff.** Keep the lanes in the second block; the first block belongs to the story and plan.
- **Teams browse the catalogue for an hour.** The project ticket is the input; the catalogue is the fallback.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations.** At the break and at the end: what surprised you in another team's project, one query plan you did not expect, one constraint that rejected something real, one interface idea worth stealing. Swap lists; do not explain. The project repositories are the record.
