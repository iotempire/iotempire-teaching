## What to expect

One project, one Task. The module is short; the work is in the room. Session 8 opens with a 10-minute course map, then teams merge, pitch the 6b project tickets, choose a story, map the must-haves, and start the slice. The checkpoint lane runs in the second block while the rest build. Sessions 9–10 are build, performance clinic, peer review, and rehearsal. Session 11 is the fair.

The dominant failure is **breadth-first building**: five half-finished features, no end-to-end path, and a demo that shows slides instead of data. Kill it in Session 8 by insisting on one vertical slice. The second failure is **scope creep dressed as ambition**: a team adding authentication in week 10 will not rehearse.

## Session 8 — kickoff and first slice

### First 90 minutes

| Time | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–5 | Register pods for checkpoint slots (TA) while the instructor starts |
| 5–15 | **The course in one page:** seven modules, what each gave, the project uses at least four |
| 15–30 | Merge two pods; each pod pitches its 6b project tickets in 60 seconds |
| 30–45 | Choose one story; answer the four questions. **Hard gate:** no person + first query = topic |
| 45–60 | One-page plan: must-haves, roles, dates; `docs/team.md` and `docs/requirements.md` |
| 60–90 | Schema sketch, first query, start the vertical slice |

**If a team cannot decide by minute 30**, send them to the [project idea bank](./project-ideas.md); do not let them browse. The project ticket is the input, not a new brainstorm.

### Second 90 minutes

| Time | What |
|---:|---|
| 0–50 | **Checkpoint 2, part 1:** two lanes (instructor + TA), five pods each × 10 min, while teams build |
| 50–90 | Build the vertical slice: schema + FK/CHECK → idempotent `ingest.py` → headline query → interface |

The checkpoint is feedback, not a grade. Pods register at the start of the day; the TA writes the notes and may suggest points/readiness and mark a recheck; the instructor records.

## Session 9 — build and performance clinic

- **First block:** Checkpoint 2, part 2 — the other ten pods, two lanes, 10 minutes each, while teams build. Registration at the start.
- **Second block:** performance clinic, 30 minutes. Three teams bring their slowest query; profile on the projector with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN`, the index that fixes it (or does not), and the before/after timing. Then build.
- The TA floats; the instructor walks and asks the three coaching questions.

## Session 10 — harden, review, rehearse

- **Stand-up:** the one must-have each team finishes today.
- **Harden:** index proof, injection sweep, trigger/constraint demonstration.
- **Peer review:** trade repositories; rebuild from the README in three commands; find one missing constraint and one unparameterized query. The reviewers commit the fixes.
- **Rehearse the fair table:** 10-minute live demo + 5-minute questions; one question per member; record the 2-minute fallback. Cut slides, not demo time.

## Session 11 — the fair

**Before class:** the first five tables are set up with laptops, posters/slides, power, and the 2-minute fallback. **During the break:** the other five set up.

| Block | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Block 1** (90 min) | Five tables × 15 min = 75 min; 15 min buffer. Instructor + TA circulate. Non-presenting students visit and write their final reflection. |
| **Break** (15 min) | The other five teams set up |
| **Block 2** (90 min) | Same format for the other five teams |

Each table gets **10 minutes live demo + 5 minutes of questions**, hard clock. The instructor and TA move together in both blocks — these are the TA's two training rounds. At every table: one thing that impressed you, then the question that matters. Use the syllabus rubric as written; do not re-derive it at the fair. The TA may suggest points and mark a recheck; the instructor records.

**Questions by role:** schema — “Which constraint would you add first, and what bad row does it stop?”; ingestion — “Run the loader twice. What guarantees no duplicates?”; query — “Show the plan. What did the index change?”; interface — “What happens when a row is missing or malformed?”; docs — “Rebuild it from the README, out loud.”; anyone — “What would you change if you started over?”

**Visitors:** visit at least three tables per block; write one thing that impressed you, one question you asked, and one concrete suggestion. Use the notes in the final reflection. The visit is part of the learning, not a break.

**After the last table:** a 10-minute retrospective — what did this course make you understand that a lecture would not? Where did the project format waste your time? What should the next cohort do differently in week one?

## TA role

- **Session 8:** register pods, help undecided teams, run a checkpoint lane.
- **Sessions 9–10:** second checkpoint lane, then float for setup and debugging.
- **Session 11:** join the instructor for both fair blocks; ask one question per member; write feedback; suggest points/readiness and mark rechecks. Never record the grade.
- A pod stuck for more than ten minutes, a student who cannot explain their part, or a broken demo is an escalation, not a rescue.

## Where it goes wrong

- **No story.** A team with no named person builds generic CRUD. Send them back to the project ticket or the idea bank.
- **Breadth-first building.** Five half-features, no end-to-end path. Force one slice in Session 8.
- **Data committed to Git.** `.db`, `.parquet`, large CSVs, and credentials stay out; the script is the deliverable.
- **One person doing everything.** Visible in the commit log within ten minutes. Intervene in Session 8, not at the fair.
- **No interface until Session 10.** Force `datasette` in Session 8; it takes one command and removes a whole class of failure.
- **The demo runs long on slides.** Rehearse the live path; cut slides, not demo time.
- **The checkpoint eats the kickoff.** Keep the lanes in the second block; the first block belongs to the story and plan.
- **Teams browse the idea bank for an hour.** The project ticket is the input; the idea bank is the fallback.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations.** At the break and at the end: what surprised you in another team's project, one query plan you did not expect, one constraint that rejected something real, one interface idea worth stealing. Swap lists; do not explain. The project repositories are the record.
