## What to expect

Three sessions of **coaching, not teaching**. Your job shifts from explaining to asking three questions over and over:

1. *"What is the first query this database must answer?"*
2. *"Can you rebuild it from your README in three commands, on a clean checkout?"*
3. *"Which must-have from Module 8 does this commit cover?"*

The dominant failure is **breadth-first building**: five half-finished features, no end-to-end path, and a demo that shows slides instead of data. Kill it in Session 8 by insisting on one vertical slice.

The second failure is **scope creep dressed as ambition**. A team that adds authentication in week 10 is a team that will not rehearse.

## Session 8 — story, plan, and the slice

- **Teams, 10 min.** Two pods merge; `docs/team.md` records the story and **who owns what**. Every member needs a defensible piece, so name it now, not in Session 10.
- **Story review, 20 min.** Teams swap stories. The reviewer's only allowed question is *"what is the first query this database must answer?"* If the answer is vague, the story is still a topic. The [story craft](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#story-craft-is-it-concrete-enough) and [story canon](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#story-canon-the-recurring-cast) notes are useful here, including the "pitchable and/or playable" test.
- **Requirements table, 15 min.** Fill it in Module 7's Step 2. This table *is* the demo script; if it is empty, there is no plan.
- **Vertical slice, rest of the session.** Schema with one foreign key and one `CHECK` → `ingest.py` that runs twice without duplicating → the headline query → the smallest interface that shows a number (`datasette db.db -o` counts).

## Sessions 9–10 — build, review, rehearse

- **Benchmark and harden.** One index with `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` before/after, an injection sweep over every user-facing field, and a trigger that rejects something on camera. These are the three things the demo will show; build them early.
- **Peer review (trade repositories).** Reviewers must actually rebuild from the README. The most common finding is a missing step that only works on the author's machine — which is exactly the lesson.
- **Rehearsal.** A timed 15-minute dry run, on the machine and the network they will use. Record a 2-minute fallback capture. **Most teams overrun on slides and under-run on the live demo**; make them cut slides, not demo time.

## Your walking-around loop

Ten minutes per team, in order, for three sessions. Ask, do not tell:

- Show me the last commit and what must-have it moves.
- Run the rebuild from scratch. How long does it take?
- Where does raw data enter, and what happens if the same file is loaded twice?
- Which query is slow, and what does the plan say?
- Who answers the "what would you change?" question in the defense?

## Where it goes wrong

- **A team with no story.** They will build a generic CRUD app. Send them back to a named person with a named problem; the [story canon](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#story-canon-the-recurring-cast) gives them a cast to borrow.
- **Data committed to Git.** `.db`, `.parquet`, and large CSVs must stay out; the *script* is the deliverable. Check `git status` in the review.
- **One person doing everything.** Visible in the commit log within ten minutes. Intervene in Session 8, not at the demo — an individual who cannot defend their part is a bad outcome for everyone.
- **No interface until Session 10.** Force `datasette` in Session 8; it takes one command and removes an entire class of failure.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations** — and the material is now their own repository. Nudge them toward: a migration step nobody understands, a command in the README that only works on one machine, and a UX surprise in the interface. Swap at each break; anything structural goes into `docs/requirements.md`.
