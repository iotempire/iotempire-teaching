## What to expect

This is the session where the old lecture said "normalize to 3NF, it is the right thing". Here, students **measure** that the normalized design is often *slower*, and then have to defend a decision. Expect genuine surprise at the benchmark table.

The concept that produces the most argument is the **functional dependency**, not the normal forms. A pod will claim `machine_id → site` is "obvious" — ask them whether the five sample rows prove it or the domain does. That distinction is the actual skill.

Also expect: `dbstat` is not available in every SQLite build. The storage query is a bonus, not a requirement; if it errors, say so and move on.

## Opening (5 minutes)

Yasmin Kaya, the operations lead, asks for the shortcut: copy `site` and `technician` onto every row and add a total. She is right about the read and wrong about the writes. Then: *"Today you do both, and you measure the difference so the decision is not a matter of taste."* That framing makes the whole session about evidence rather than dogma.

## Running the studio

- **Task A (study, 20 min).** The five-row extract is the warm-up; the real work is on the 200,000-row table. Have pods mark each FD as *domain fact* or *observed accident* — that column is where the thinking shows.
- **Task B (share, 10 min).** Compare FD lists across pods. Every pod will have one FD the others do not, and arguing it out is the point.
- **Task 1 (dependencies and anomalies).** Ask for the *count of rows* an update touches. A number is convincing; "many rows" is not.
- **Task 2 (3NF).** The integrity check — `flat_rows == normalized_rows` — is the deliverable. If they differ, the decomposition lost data; that is the lesson.
- **Task 3 (benchmark).** Fill the table honestly, including the write cost and the storage. The denormalized design usually wins the read and loses the write; if your numbers say otherwise, that is a finding, not a mistake.
- **Task 4 (decide and defend).** Force a one-sentence decision *with the number that justifies it*. "It depends" is not an answer; "we chose X because the dashboard runs 4×/hour and the write path 40×/minute" is.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Foreign keys are off**, so inserting into `job_part` with a missing part *succeeds*. They will not notice unless you ask them to check. Link it back to Module 4.
- **Comparing the wrong thing.** The normalized dashboard joins four tables; the denormalized one scans one. That is not apples to apples — and noticing that is a higher-level insight than the timing itself.
- **Copying prices into the report table** and then changing a price: the report now lies. Demonstrate it once; it is the whole point of the session.
- **`SUM` over a text price.** SQLite will coerce it and the number will look right — until the sort order surprises them.

## Read up on

- Functional dependencies and candidate keys — the formal definition, not just the examples.
- Third normal form and BCNF — enough to explain why 3NF is the practical default.
- Denormalization as an engineering decision; the [resource bank](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#watching-instead-of-reading) has a video if a refresher is faster.
- `dbstat`, in case a student asks how storage is measured.

## Harvest

**Collections, not explanations.** Nudge them toward: `printf('%.2f', …)` storing money as text, the `CROSS JOIN` that turns 20,000 × 10 into 200,000 rows, and any functional dependency they disagree about. Swap at the break and at the end.
