## What to expect

**There is no pod study any more.** The first 15 minutes are a **skim**, not a read: students look at the seven rounds and the theory boxes, then start Round 1. Expect the room to be at five different rounds within an hour — that is the design, not a failure.

The gates are the only synchronisation. Two minutes, neighbour to neighbour, no instructor. Your job is to walk, listen at the gates, and answer only when a neighbour cannot. If you find yourself explaining normal forms to a whole table, you have become the lecture this format replaced.

## The rounds — what to check at each gate

| Round | What “good” looks like | Common wrong answer | Intervene when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · See it** | FDs marked *domain* vs *observed*; diagram 2 has a junction table | inventing `machine_id → technician` | no junction table appears in the second diagram |
| **2 · Break it** | Three statements; the delete case shows a fact vanishing | “insert anomaly = insert a duplicate” | they only rename a row |
| **3 · Build it** | 200000 = 200000; `part` has 10 rows | counts differ because the decomposition lost or duplicated rows | the two counts differ |
| **4 · Race it** | Both plans captured; timings in ms; ratio noted | “denormalized is much faster” with no numbers | no `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` output |
| **5 · Price it** | 1 vs 4,000 rows; storage ≈ 2.7×; the index-drop finding | timing a single-row insert | they skip dropping the index |
| **6 · Decide** | Memo cites their own numbers and names a staleness strategy | “denormalize everything” | no plan for keeping the copy fresh |
| **7 · Harvest** | Curiosities with sources, swapped | — | — |

The gates are where you assess **for feedback**, not for points: “not yet” is a complete answer, and the round can be revisited.

## The gate board

Pods down one side, rounds across the top. A tick when a pod has passed a gate. It costs nothing and it shows you the room at a glance: a pod stuck on Round 1 for 25 minutes needs a nudge; a pod on Round 5 is fine.

## Where it goes wrong

- **Foreign keys are off**, so an insert into `job_part` with a missing part *succeeds* and creates a dangling reference. Round 3 now asks them to turn the pragma on once. Link it back to Module 4.
- **The index on the report table hurts.** `SCAN report_jobpart USING INDEX ix_report_machine` walks the index and then fetches 200,000 rows from the table. Dropping it makes the query faster. If nobody finds this, ask them to.
- **Milliseconds, not 40 seconds.** The story is about a production dataset; the lab is 200,000 rows. Students will measure ~20–30 ms and think they did it wrong. Say it before they do.
- **Comparing the wrong thing.** The normalized dashboard joins four tables; the denormalized one scans one. That is not apples to apples — and noticing that is a higher-level insight than the timing itself.
- **Copying prices into the report table** and then changing a price: the report now lies. Demonstrate it once; it is the whole point of the session.
- **`SUM` over a text price.** SQLite will coerce it and the number will look right — until the sort order surprises them.
- **Skipping the skim.** A pod that starts at Round 3 without the primer will build the wrong tables and come back confused. Two minutes of “what will I produce?” saves twenty.

## Read up on

- Functional dependencies and candidate keys — the formal definition, not just the examples.
- Third normal form and BCNF — enough to explain why 3NF is the practical default.
- Denormalization as an engineering decision; the [resource bank](../modules/Z-resources-bank.md#watching-instead-of-reading) has a video if a refresher is faster.
- `dbstat`, in case a student asks how storage is measured.

## Harvest

Round 7, at the end: **collections, not explanations.** Nudge them toward `printf('%.2f', …)` storing money as text, the `CROSS JOIN` that turns 20,000 × 10 into 200,000 rows, the report-table plan, and any functional dependency they disagree about. Swap lists with the nearest pod; unresolved entries go to `#sql-questions`.
